theorem-developmentcurrent

Purpose

This source-neutral module turns blockwise finite-cardinality equalities into a selected block-preserving equivalence, its radical-label partition, and the induced equivalences on block/radical fibres.

Mathematical statement and assumptions

Equal finite block-fibre cardinalities give a noncanonical block-preserving matching. If the source fibre is a singleton, any such equivalence takes its distinguished element to the prescribed element in the target fibre.

Argument or construction

Choose finite equivalences for corresponding block fibres from cardinality equalities and combine them. If a distinguished source fibre is a singleton, every such equivalence must take its point to the prescribed target point.

External assumptions and dependencies

Finite source and target fibres with equal blockwise cardinalities. The special normalization theorem additionally assumes its source fibre is a singleton and the target distinguished point lies in that block.
